# Paper summary: Sarkar & Khan (2021)

Sarkar, S.K., & Khan, M.M.H. (2021). Impact of COVID-19 on PM2.5 pollution in
fastest-growing megacity Dhaka, Bangladesh. Disaster Medicine and Public Health
Preparedness, 1–4. https://doi.org/10.1017/dmp.2021.131

## What they studied
The authors measured how much the COVID-19 lockdown reduced PM2.5 in Dhaka. They used
hourly PM2.5 from the U.S. Embassy monitor (AirNow), January 2017 to August 2020,
converted to daily means. They split 2020 into pre-lockdown, lockdown (24 March – 30 May)
and post-lockdown periods, and compared each with the average of the same dates in
2017–2019.

## What they found
During the lockdown, mean PM2.5 fell from 63.2 to 56.1 µg/m³, a drop of 7.15 µg/m³
(−11.3%). The median fell by about 17%. After the lockdown there was no difference (+0.4%).

## Weaknesses
They did not control for weather. If rain, wind or temperature in spring 2020 were
different from earlier years, part of the drop could be due to weather. They also describe
the result as significant, but the p-value was 0.10 (above the usual 0.05) and the 95%
confidence interval crosses zero, so the drop is not statistically confirmed.

## How it helps my project
This is a calibration point. Even with offices, transport and most industry closed,
PM2.5 fell by only about 7 µg/m³ (about 11%), and even that is not statistically clear.
A single crackdown, such as on brick kilns or construction dust, will probably have a
much smaller effect than the lockdown, perhaps only a few µg/m³. So I need to control for
weather and use a longer baseline (2016–2025), or the effect will be lost in the noise.
Their "same calendar window, current vs historical" comparison is also a simple starting
template for my crackdown analysis.
