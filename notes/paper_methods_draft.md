# Materials and Methods (draft)

## Air quality data
Hourly PM2.5 concentrations were obtained from the beta attenuation monitor operated by the U.S. Embassy in Baridhara, Dhaka, through the OpenAQ API (version 3). The monitor appears in OpenAQ as two consecutive records: sensor 4680 (StateAir, 11 March 2016 to 9 November 2016) and sensor 24434 (AirNow, 10 November 2016 to 24 March 2025). The two records were downloaded month by month, with automatic retries when the server limited requests, and joined into a single series in Dhaka local time (UTC+6), giving 72,035 hourly records.

The data were cleaned in four steps. First, values below zero and values of 985 µg/m³ or above were removed; 985 µg/m³ is an error code of the monitor, and these values produced an impossible monthly mean of almost 1,000 µg/m³ in September 2018 (43 hours). Second, readings repeated unchanged for eight or more consecutive hours were treated as instrument faults (none were found). Third, isolated spikes were removed when a reading exceeded 500 µg/m³ and was more than three times the median of the surrounding 24 hours (25 hours). Fourth, 5,288 hours with no recorded value were excluded. The final series contained 66,679 valid hours, about 84% of the study period. A small number of zero readings were set to 1 µg/m³, the approximate detection limit of the monitor. Following Sarwar et al. (2023), daily means were calculated only for days with at least 18 valid hours, giving 2,478 days.

## Weather data
Hourly temperature, relative humidity, precipitation, wind speed and wind direction for 23.80°N, 90.42°E were obtained from the Open-Meteo historical weather API, which is based on the ERA5 reanalysis, for 1 January 2016 to 31 December 2025.

## Weather model
To separate the effect of weather from other changes in PM2.5, a gradient boosting model (HistGradientBoostingRegressor, scikit-learn; 300 iterations, learning rate 0.05) was trained to predict the logarithm of daily mean PM2.5. The predictors were daily mean temperature, daily temperature range, mean relative humidity, daily precipitation, precipitation on the previous day, mean wind speed, wind speed on the previous day, the east–west and north–south components of the wind, day of the year and day of the week. Predictions were made with leave-one-year-out cross-validation: each year was predicted by a model trained only on the other years, so no day was predicted by a model that had seen it. The model explained 80% of the variation in daily log PM2.5 (R² = 0.80). For each day, weather-adjusted PM2.5 was defined as the percentage by which the observed value differed from the model's expected value.

## Enforcement timeline
Enforcement actions were identified by searching English-language Bangladeshi news sites (The Daily Star, Dhaka Tribune, Prothom Alo English, The Financial Express, The Business Standard, United News of Bangladesh, The Daily Observer and banglanews24) for court orders, mobile court drives, brick kiln demolitions and new rules concerning air pollution in and around Dhaka. The date of each action was determined from the article's publication date and its wording ("on Monday", "today", "yesterday"), and checked against the day of the week. Print articles from The Daily Star are published online the evening before the print date, so "yesterday" in these articles refers to the online publication date; this was confirmed against other newspapers in six cases. An action was classed as verified only when two independent outlets agreed on its date. Actions were classed by type: court order, court summons, mobile court, demolition, rule, or announcement.

The search produced 28 actions between January 2019 and October 2025, of which 14 were verified. Two verified actions (31 January and 5 February 2023) were excluded because no monitor data were available in the following month, leaving 12. Actions less than 30 days apart were merged into a single cluster dated by the first action, giving 10 clusters (Table 2).

## Calibration
Before testing enforcement actions, the method was tested on periods when activity in Dhaka is known to have fallen. Eid dates (2016–2024) were taken from a public holiday calendar; three Eid ul-Fitr dates (2016, 2018 and 2024) were corrected to the Bangladesh date, which followed moon sighting one day later. For each Eid, the effect was the mean weather-adjusted PM2.5 from one day before to three days after Eid, minus the mean on normal days (30 to 8 days before and 10 to 30 days after), excluding Eids with fewer than 3 days in the holiday window or fewer than 15 normal days (13 of 18 Eids were included). The same measurement was repeated on 1,000 random dates at least 40 days from any Eid, and the average Eid effect was compared with 5,000 random sets of 13 dates. The COVID-19 general holiday (26 March to 30 May 2020) was compared with 1 February to 7 March and 15 June to 15 July 2020.

## Main test
The main analysis was pre-registered on the Open Science Framework on 27 October 2026, before it was run (https://osf.io/6bsek). Days within an Eid window and the COVID-19 holiday were excluded. For each cluster, the effect was the mean weather-adjusted PM2.5 on days 1 to 30 after the action minus the mean on days 1 to 30 before it, excluding the day of the action; a cluster required at least 15 days of data on each side. The effects were averaged across clusters. To test whether this average could arise by chance, the same calculation was repeated for 1,000 sets of 10 random dates, each at least 40 days from any enforcement action, Eid or the COVID-19 holiday. The one-sided p-value for H1 was the share of random sets with an average as low as or lower than the observed average.

H2 compared the effect on days 1 to 10 with the effect on days 21 to 30. Pre-registered robustness checks excluded court summonses, dated one cluster to an alternative start (4 January 2021), and used 15-day and 60-day windows. For windows shorter than 30 days, the minimum data requirement was set to half the window length, because 15 days of data cannot be reached in a 10-day window; this is a deviation from the pre-registration.

## Exploratory analyses
The following checks were not pre-registered. Pre-event trends were measured as the change in weather-adjusted PM2.5 from days 60–31 to days 30–1 before each action. Levels before and after each action were compared with the typical level before random dates. A month-matched placebo (2,000 sets) drew random dates from the same calendar months as the actions. Raw PM2.5 in the 30 days before each action was compared with the average for that month.

## Software, data and AI use
Analyses were carried out in Python (pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn and Matplotlib) in Google Colab. All data, code and the enforcement timeline are available at https://github.com/prantiqueroy/dhaka-air-enforcement.
